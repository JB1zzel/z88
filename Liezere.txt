If last week you had told me I was about to fall in love
with a semi-autobiographical ice fishing simulator for the
ZX Spectrum, you would have been both deeply prophetic and
eerily correct.

Made up of a series of simple minigames, *Liezere* is a
cosy fishing simulator that is much more than the sum of
its parts. The author describes it as "an ice fishing
simulator based on real events when my friends and I went
ice fishing during our high school winter break sometime
around the '90s." It nails this vibe so well that it is
almost a work of art.

Let me be clear about something: I have never been a
Latvian teenager. I have never, in the 1990s or otherwise,
crept out of a warm house before dawn, met friends in the
crystalline dark, walked across a frozen lake with the snow
crunching underfoot, drilled a hole through a metre of ice,
and spent the day staring into it, waiting. I need to
remind myself of this fact more than you might think.
Because playing Liezere, I remember it. The game is so
evocative, so perfectly pitched in its sensory details,
that it has lodged itself in my mind as a false memory.
That is not good game design. That is sorcery.

The first task is to pick an area on the lake to fish.
Then you mash some buttons to drill a hole. Then you mash
other buttons to get a bite, before finally mashing
buttons—slowly this time—to reel in the fish. The game
then tells you what you caught, and based on that
information, the whole thing starts again. The fish you
have just caught offers a clue as to where the next,
bigger fish might be. The goal is to catch 'lunkers'—
exceptionally big fish, often considered a trophy catch
due to their size and weight.

You have limited time to find and catch your lunker. If
you fail, you go home, give up fishing, and get blind
drunk. If you succeed, you go home, eat the fish, give
the scraps to your cat, and get blind drunk.

The game has a campaign; you have to deal with different
challenges each day. These include hangovers, catching
multiple lunkers, and other surprises.

The game's genius is threefold. First, the sumptuous
presentation: gloriously smooth animations complement a
charming art style and groovy music. Second, the change
of pace when button mashing—luring the fish needs quick
mashing, while reeling the fish in is slower. Add this to
the time pressure of a waning day, and you have a game
that is more addictive than expected. Third, the way the
story unfolds feels deeply evocative. Feeding the cat,
falling into a drunken stupor, drilling holes in the ice—
these are not the usual aspects of a ZX Spectrum game,
but it is these little touches that let you know you are
playing something rather special.

I have never gone ice fishing. But this game makes me
mourn that fact. It has instilled in me a deep, aching
nostalgia for a past that is not mine, for winters I
never knew, for friendships I never had. And that, I
think, is quite an achievement for a little Spectrum
game. Liezere is the lunker. Reel it in.
