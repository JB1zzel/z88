Everest Adventure. FREE        Why climb a mountain? Because
it's there" - as George Mallory famously replied. Personally,
I'd do it for the views. Gorgeous panoramas of nature at its
most untamed, all framed beneath that enigmatic and elusive
blue stuff we call the sky. Of course, I live in the UK, so
there aren't so many mountains, and often when I climb them,
rather than blue sky, it's gray drizzle. Still, I keep doing
it. I'm past the 'all the gear, no idea' phase and into 'all
the gear, pushing on through the rain, up, up, up!

So, as an avid walker, going on an Everest Adventure does
sound appealing. Especially when it can be done from the
comfort of my armchair, on my beloved ZX Spectrum. No rain
in sight, just a nice cup of tea. Feet up on the pouffe.

Everest Adventure is a graphical text adventure that sets
you the challenge of climbing the highest mountain in the
world. Regardless of your motivation, to succeed you will
need determination, skill, knowledge (the idea part), and
of course - the gear! And it's that last part that is
immediately tricky. While you start in your yellow tent at
base camp, raring to go, you are missing key equipment that
will make your trip possible - you first task is to explore
base camp, meet inhabitants and find your gear.

Right from the off, you are greeted with a striking visual
feast. The graphical aspect of the game is no afterthought,
but a fundamental. The colours are often paired back, chunky
black outlines do not simply serve to mask the Spectrum's
limitations, but allow the game to flaunt what can be
achieved with an artful sensibility and a will to keep
things simple. So the game has what I promised a good climb
should have - a spectacular treat for the eyes. Everest
Adventure looks gorgeous.

The parser is the downfall of many intrepid text
adventurers. QAOP + M for fire has become a Spectrum
standard for platform games in recent years - gone are the
awkward keys of the Spectrum's inception - Symbol Shift and
M: I'm looking at you, Ant Attack. Similar strides towards
standardisation have taken place in the text adventure
world. Thankfully, Everest Adventure feels thoroughly
modern, and anyone who is a fan of the home-brew text
adventures of recent years should have no trouble at all.
Beginners will also appreciate its story telling and
guidance. I always felt like I knew what I was supposed to
be doing and what options I had to accomplish my goals.

So why climb a mountain in a game? Because it's there - but
also because the rain isn't. Everest Adventure lets me have
the views, the challenge, and the triumph, all without
leaving the pouffe. The graphics are a treat, the parser is
a dream, and the only grey drizzle is the one outside my
window. I started this review talking about gear and
determination. Turns out, all I really needed was a ZX
Spectrum and a good cup of tea. Up, up, up - from my
armchair. George Mallory, eat your heart out.
